# Sans-Serif Font Design

Use this skill for sans-specific drawing decisions. Keep provenance, coordinates, coverage, and delivery in the [production workflow](../font-production-workflow/SKILL.md); use [design metrics](../font-design-metrics/SKILL.md) for shared measurements and [outline QA](../font-outline-qa/SKILL.md) for every glyph's visual and SVG review.

## Establish the intended construction

Read the brief's proportions, contrast, rhythm, and terminal character before choosing geometric, grotesque, humanist, rounded, or mixed construction. Sans-serif does not imply perfect circles, equal-width characters, mechanically monoline strokes, or closed apertures.

Use `H O n o` with an open form, a diagonal, and a narrow letter to establish the relationships. Compare widths, round squareness, shoulder structure, aperture direction, and terminal cuts. Preserve the source concept's recognizable choices, including single- or double-storey `a/g`, rather than replacing unfamiliar details with a generic sans.

## Balance strokes by their visible effect

Set related vertical, horizontal, round-side, and diagonal stroke families using the shared metrics guidance. Compare apparent weight at the intended size; nominally equal thickness can produce unequal texture. A circle with a uniformly inset counter is a useful sketch, not proof of balanced round strokes.

Measure diagonals perpendicular to their edges. Compare `V/W/X` and `v/w/x/y` with upright controls, adding thickness where they look weak while keeping counters open. Test each family in context rather than applying one global percentage. In monoline designs, preserve the appearance of even weight through local compensation; in contrasted designs, preserve the intended contrast.

Control dark joins in `n/m/h`, `a`, `k`, and diagonal vertices. Narrowing a join or adjusting the counter can relieve accumulated mass without thinning an entire stem. Keep such relief smooth and appropriately subtle for the design and viewing size; do not add decorative ink traps by default.

## Draw apertures, bowls, and terminals as a system

Compare open forms such as `c/e/s` for aperture width and terminal direction. A mathematically aligned terminal may make one opening look pinched; account for the surrounding curve and intended reading size. Retain deliberate open or closed character rather than enforcing one universal aperture.

Inspect round-to-straight transitions and shoulders for bulges, flat spots, or abrupt changes of curvature. Use the actual outer and inner contours to locate the cause; scaling a whole bowl often changes width, weight, and counter tension at once. Related `b/d/p/q` need consistent logic without mandatory mirroring.

Choose flat, angled, rounded, or flared terminals from the brief, with coherent tangent directions and corner treatment. A rounded sans needs designed terminals and joins; globally rounding every outline corner can inflate endpoints and reduce counters.

Compare round and pointed extrema with flat controls for apparent height and baseline alignment. Record supported overshoots and width corrections through the shared metrics contract instead of forcing all bounds to match exactly.

## Preserve differentiation at use size

Check `I/l/1`, `O/0`, `rn/m`, and `c/e` in words and numbers appropriate to the use case. Introduce distinctions that belong to the design's terminal, proportion, and aperture vocabulary; do not add arbitrary marks to satisfy a checklist.

Size and position `i/j` dots by apparent weight and separation from their stems. Round, square, and diamond dots of equal bounding width carry different visual mass. Inspect punctuation and accents alongside these details so one family does not appear accidental or underweight.

Review repeated straights, rounds, and mixed forms in compiled text at the intended size. Once outlines are stable, use [spacing and kerning](../font-spacing-kerning/SKILL.md) to resolve rhythm; do not distort widths solely to conceal a spacing problem.
